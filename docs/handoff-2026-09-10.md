And, found by the generator rather than a reader (D-046): eight refusals wider than their shape in one day, plus one stale-variable bug. **The two halves are one rule: a refusal is exactly as wide as its shape.** A hostile reader finds the narrow fix (it leaves an overclaim on the page); only the generator finds the wide one (it leaves no page). The regeneration is therefore a second instrument, and its failures are evidence about the lint.

**Practices now standing:** every refusal carries an admit-case, seeded from the prompt's own examples, tested on a paragraph (`test_every_refusal_admits_the_sentence_it_must_admit`); a fixed text on the page is tested against the computation it labels; a ruleset change names the regenerated sheet that shows it (D-038 — a closure was once logged and never written); commits gate on the test runner's exit status, and patch scripts write each file as they go.

**The open question the ninth reader posed:** the reading has stopped overclaiming and started reciting — its first paragraphs are the register read aloud, and no rule can refuse recitation without the sentence carrying something not on the sheet, which is the hole the lint declares.
